    What does this actually do to my audio?
    • Measures how loud your video is with the industry-standard loudness meter (LUFS, ITU-R BS.1770), including "true peaks", the ones that appear between digital samples.
    • Rumble cut (talking/music modes): removes low thumps, handling noise and desk bumps you can't really hear but that eat up headroom.
    • Voice clean-up (talking mode): gently lowers room noise between sentences, evens out loud and quiet phrases, and tames harsh "s" sounds. Optional noise reduction removes steady hiss and hum.
    • Compression (talking/music modes): brings loud moments down and quiet moments up, so viewers don't have to touch the volume. Talking mode does more of it than music mode.
    • Loudness to target: sets the overall level to what the platform expects, so you're not quieter than everyone else in the feed, and not so loud the platform turns you down.
    • Peak limiter: stops the loudest instants from clipping or crackling after the platform re-compresses your upload. The result is measured again after encoding and re-done if any peak is over the ceiling. The limiter is never allowed to squash hard; if reaching the target would need that, the tool stops a little short and tells you.
    • Your picture is copied bit-for-bit, with no quality loss, unless you choose "Convert to Full HD". Then it is re-encoded at a high bitrate on your device's video hardware, keeping the original frame rate. The recording location (GPS) that phones store in videos is removed.

    LUFS is a loudness unit that matches how loud things sound to people. More negative means quieter: −14 is louder than −16. Loudness range (LU) is how much the volume swings between quiet and loud parts. For talking videos, 4–8 is comfortable on a phone.

    Powered by FFmpeg (WebAssembly). The first run downloads the audio engine (about 10–30 MB); your video itself never leaves this device.
